CareGrid pilot · Phoenix, AZ · July 2023 heatwave

When the power goes out in a heatwave, who keeps their oxygen?

Eight elderly households and a six-bed care facility share a 60 kWp solar array and a community battery. The grid fails on a real July 2023 afternoon. Each controller decides, hour by hour, what gets the scarce energy. Change the outage and see how each one copes.

The utility's restoration estimate is always 24 h. The controllers don't know the real length.
The shield keeps enough battery to run every medical device until this time.

What the full experiment found

28 outage start days × 4 real outage lengths × with/without warning, plus sweeps. It includes two planning controllers (MPC) that are too heavy to run in this page, and a perfect-foresight optimum as the upper bound.

Medical device-hours without power, by actual outage length (mean of 28 start days, no warning)

Every controller is told restoration will take 24 h. MPC plans with a perfect weather forecast. The perfect-foresight optimum also knows the true outage length, and its device on/off decisions are relaxed, so it is an upper bound and not something a real controller can reach.

Model, data and limits

Weather: Open-Meteo historical archive (ERA5 reanalysis), hourly, Phoenix, 30 Jun–3 Aug 2023. Household base load: NREL ResStock 2021 (AMY2018), Maricopa County single-family homes, July average by hour. Buildings: 1R1C grey-box thermal model calibrated to ResStock July cooling energy (model 55.9 kWh/day for July 2023 vs 51.1 kWh/day for cooler July 2018). Medical devices: oxygen concentrators 350 W, CPAP 60 W (nights), insulin/medication fridges 120–150 W, ventilator 150 W, power-wheelchair charger 300 W. Heat thresholds use NWS heat-index bands (26.7 °C caution, 32.2 °C extreme caution); Phoenix humidity is low enough that air temperature stands in for heat index.

Limits: simulation only, nine synthetic households, hourly steps, one city and one month, no distribution-network constraints, constant grid carbon factor (eGRID AZNM assumption). The controllers are rule-based and optimisation-based; the constrained-RL policy proposed for CareGrid is future work, and the shield is designed to wrap it.
